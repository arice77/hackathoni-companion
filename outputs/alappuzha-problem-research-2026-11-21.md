# Alappuzha field research for HackaThoni

21 November 2026. This brief covers Alappuzha town and selected places in the district, especially the Alappuzha side of Kuttanad. Survey respondents, administrative records and news reports each tell us something different. The product ideas below are hypotheses drawn from those sources. No new interviews or site observations were conducted for this brief.

## Three problems to investigate first

### 1. Paddy farmers preparing a crop-loss application in Kuttanad

**Who and what.** Paddy farmers and the people who help them prepare their applications. CANALPY's August 2018 post-flood assessment surveyed **2,200 households across 14 panchayats** in the Alappuzha part of Kuttanad. Manorama's Alappuzha bureau reported fresh paddy damage in the Pulincunnoo Krishi Bhavan area on **28 August 2026**, including farmers worried about compensation after they had been unable to complete insurance in time. [CANALPY rapid assessment](https://www.cps.iitb.ac.in/livinglaballeppey/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Rapid-Flood-Impact-Assessment-in-Kuttanad-Region-new-1.pdf) · [Manorama News field report](https://www.manoramanews.com/nattuvartha/south/alappuzha/2026/08/28/kuttanad-farmers-flood-crisis.amp.html)

**Evidence about the task.** A 2025 study of 120 farmers in selected panchayats reported that 68% of its sample cited complex documentation and 55% cited limited digital literacy across the schemes studied. These are findings for the pooled sample, which the authors group under Alappuzha, Kottayam and Kuttanad. They do not establish the rate for the district or for AIMS users. Kerala Agriculture's March 2026 manual shows the existing application screens. The Kerala e-services dashboard lists 60,368 crop-loss-assistance applications in Alappuzha from September 2019 to its May 2026 snapshot. This is a count of filings. The dashboard does not diagnose the reason for delays or establish that approved applications were paid. [Farmer study, tables 1 and 3](https://www.extensionjournal.com/uploads/archives/8-10-52-362.pdf) · [AIMS user manual](https://keralaagriculture.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Crop-Insurance-Consolidated-UM.pdf) · [Kerala service dashboard](https://dashboard.kerala.gov.in/e-services/service.php?fcgzGljlqlQrXcSXWDDRKHpqTpMzsq=ZGlkPTEmc2VyPUFzc2lzdGFuY2UrZm9yK0Nyb3ArTG9zcytkdWUrdG8rTmF0dXJhbCtDYWxhbWl0eQ%3D%3D)

**Overnight trial.** Give a farmer or facilitator a Malayalam voice guide over a three-screen training form based on the manual. One synthetic paddy-loss case ends with a reviewable draft. The companion points to the actual field or upload control and checks whether the next screen appeared. Primary track Place & People, with Voice & Language as a second fit. This is the closest match to the tested reader-and-pointer interaction.

**Decision question.** Ask a farmer or Krishi Bhavan/Akshaya facilitator to walk through their last application with sample or redacted details. Where did it stall? A problem with documents, evidence preparation or field navigation supports this idea. Delayed payments, crop loss itself or a missed insurance window call for a different solution. Use synthetic records in the demo and leave official eligibility and filing decisions to the relevant services.

### 2. Interstate contract workers reconstructing work and pay

**Who and what.** A *Disasters* study based on 2021 interviews records a worker advocate's experience handling 10 wage disputes in a year working in Alappuzha, of which three were resolved. The advocate described asking workers to note employer, agreed wages and pay received in a notebook. The same interview describes an Alleppey fish cold-storage worker who needed translation assistance during a dispute and lacked records. This is one advocate's caseload and an account of a workaround. [Original study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/disa.12614)

**Survey context.** The Kerala Institute of Labour and Employment completed 5,720 interviews across Kerala in 2019, including 401 respondents in Alappuzha. It reports that 84.9% of surveyed workers statewide did not know how to register a Labour Department complaint. The state Economic Review lists 26,040 Athidhi registrations for Alappuzha as of 20 November 2025. Registrations measure program use and neither source estimates current district wage disputes. [KILE survey, tables 5.1 and 5.15](https://kile.kerala.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/ISM-Coffe-table_compressed-1-1.pdf) · [Kerala Economic Review 2025, appendix 7.1.14](https://spb.kerala.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-01/ER%202025%20Volume%202%20Eng%20final.pdf)

**Overnight trial.** A worker records a shift and a partial payment by voice in a language a local worker or helper identifies. The app reads back the date and amounts, highlights the one field to correct, and saves a private chronology. The worker can export a bilingual summary if they choose. Demo three synthetic entries, a corrected amount and the resulting balance. Voice is central to the workflow. Primary track Place & People or Voice & Language, depending on the pitch.

**Decision question.** See how one worker or welfare volunteer records and reconstructs payments today. Would a voice diary fit their phone access, privacy and sharing preferences? The cited interview establishes the documentation problem and notebook workaround. It does not establish demand for an app or which language this person wants to use.

### 3. Houseboat waste receipts and pickup records at Punnamada

**Who and what.** Kerala Agricultural University researchers interviewed **60 randomly selected Punnamada Lake houseboat owners**, and their October 2026 paper ranked inadequate waste-management infrastructure first among concerns. Manorama's December 2025 reporting describes a collection suspension and rubbish accumulating at Punnamada Finishing Point. These sources establish infrastructure and collection problems. [Punnamada owner survey](https://journalajaees.com/index.php/AJAEES/article/view/3035) · [December 2025 reporting](https://www.manoramaonline.com/district-news/alappuzha/2025/12/30/alappuzha-houseboat-waste-crisis.html)

**Evidence about the task.** DTPC's 2025 tender describes the collector issuing a receipt and maintaining a daily register. Its September 2026 agreement format describes segregated organic/plastic waste handover at a designated point, a collector-issued receipt, and monthly submission for verification. The survey's waste score of 98 is an index, not a percentage of owners. The documents do not establish that receipts are lost, that all points are currently operating, or that the process is paper-only. [DTPC tender, p. 6](https://www.dtpcalappuzha.com/uploads/tenders/houseboatwastecollectionwebsiteadvtz2025-20250509143754596540.pdf#page=6) · [DTPC agreement, clauses 6, 13 and 14](https://www.dtpcalappuzha.com/uploads/tenders/houseboat-waste-management-notice-form-agreement-20260909110302516455.pdf)

**Overnight trial.** One operator, one collection point and three sample collector-issued receipts. Compare the receipts to the operator's handoff checklist. The overlay points to a missing or mismatched record and the operator confirms the corrected monthly packet. Use a sample register only if the proposed user could actually access it. Track Place & People.

**Decision question.** Ask a crew member or operator to describe the last handoff and show the receipt format if comfortable. When pickup fails, is the hard part finding who is collecting, getting waste to the point, or keeping proof? Build the receipts idea only if record friction is real. Software has little influence over an absent collection vehicle or an infrastructure shortage.

It draws on land-use, household and traffic surveys and contains ward-level material on drainage, transport and waste. Its scope is Alappuzha municipality, and older survey values should be dated when cited.

## How to pick on the boat

The crop-loss coach has the strongest fit with recognizing controls, pointing to the next step and checking the outcome. The wage diary has the clearest documented workaround. The houseboat option has the most accessible local interview. The ferry option has the lowest dependency on private records and partner access.

Spend the first conversation asking for a real episode. “What happened the last time you did this?” “What document, message, or screen did you use?” “Where did it stop, and what did you do next?” That can distinguish a repeatable problem the software can improve from a convenient feature idea. One interview can ground the demo and suggest the next test. It cannot establish district-wide prevalence.
